# Smart Parking — User Research

## About This Document

This document summarizes initial observations gathered from conversations with employees who use the office parking area.

The information below represents exploratory research.

It must NOT automatically be treated as requirements.

---

# Research Round 1

## Participants

Conversations were conducted with:

- 5 employees who go to the office 3 or more times per week;
- 3 employees who go to the office 1 or 2 times per week;
- 2 employees who normally use public transportation.

Total:

10 participants.

This is a small sample and should not be considered representative of the entire organization.

---

# Main Observations

## 1. The Biggest Concern Happens Before Leaving Home

Several participants said they would like to know in advance whether there is a good chance of finding parking.

Uncertainty was often mentioned as more frustrating than the time spent searching itself.

### Evidence

6 of the 8 participants who regularly use a car mentioned this problem spontaneously.

---

## 2. Some Employees Arrive Earlier Because of Parking

Some participants said they change their arrival time to increase the chance of finding a parking space.

This may indicate that the problem influences behavior even before the person arrives at the office.

---

## 3. Reservation Sounds Interesting, but Opinions Differ

Some participants said they would like to reserve a parking space.

Others were concerned that people might reserve a space and then not show up.

There is still no consensus on:

- whether reservation is necessary;
- how long a reservation should remain valid;
- when it should expire;
- how no-shows should be handled.

---

## 4. Most Participants Were Not Interested in Choosing a Specific Space

For most participants, knowing that parking is available appears to be more important than choosing a specific parking space.

One participant expressed interest in choosing spaces closer to the entrance.

Accessibility needs should be investigated separately.

---

## 5. Real-Time Information Was Considered Valuable

Several participants said they would like to check parking conditions before starting their trip.

We still do not know what level of accuracy would be considered acceptable.

We also do not know how frequently the data would need to be updated.

---

# Identified Needs

Based on the initial research:

### Need 1

> I want to understand the parking situation before leaving home.

### Need 2

> I want to reduce the time wasted searching for a parking space.

### Need 3

> I want to trust that the information shown reasonably reflects the real situation.

### Need 4

> I want using the system to be simpler than simply trying to find a parking space on my own.

---

# Observed Pain Points

- uncertainty before the trip;
- time wasted searching for spaces;
- unnecessary changes to arrival time;
- lack of reliable information;
- perception of empty spaces that cannot be used.

---

# Ideas Mentioned by Users

During the interviews, some ideas were suggested:

- show the number of available parking spaces;
- allow reservations;
- notify users when a parking space becomes available;
- show occupancy by area;
- show a forecast for a specific time;
- allow quick cancellation of a reservation.

IMPORTANT:

These ideas are NOT requirements.

They are suggestions made during research and must be evaluated against:

- user needs;
- business goals;
- feasibility;
- risks;
- other evidence.

---

# Contradictions Found

## Reservation

Some users said:

> “If I could reserve a space, I would know that driving to the office is worthwhile.”

Others raised concerns:

> “If everyone reserves and half of them do not show up, the parking area may appear full even when it is not.”

Therefore:

We should not assume that “adding reservations” is automatically the correct solution.

---

# Current Assumptions

The following statements are hypotheses, not proven facts:

- users will use the system before leaving home;
- real-time availability will reduce search time;
- reservation will increase predictability;
- users will cancel reservations they do not intend to use;
- the available sensors are accurate enough.

These hypotheses need to be validated.

---

# Questions for the Next Research Round

We need to investigate:

1. How far in advance would users check parking availability?
2. Would availability information alone be enough?
3. In which situations would reservation actually be necessary?
4. How long should a reservation remain valid?
5. What should happen in the event of a no-show?
6. What level of accuracy do users expect?
7. Are there specific accessibility needs?
8. How should visitors use the parking area?
9. What information would users be willing to provide about their vehicles?

---

# Current Conclusion

There is initial evidence that:

- predictability is a relevant problem;
- time spent searching for parking creates frustration;
- advance information may provide value.

There is still NOT enough evidence to conclude that:

- reservations are definitely necessary;
- users need to choose a specific parking space;
- we should store detailed vehicle data;
- notifications are necessary;
- availability forecasting is technically feasible.
